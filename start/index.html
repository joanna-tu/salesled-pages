Meeting card Part one of three

Example: Cartesa meeting Ingrid Solheim, CDO at Nordvik Outdoor. All fictional.

The bet
A new CDO with a public speed mandate is rebuilding digital right now, and the platform is the bottleneck her own CFO named. This quarter, not next.
Ask · menu, not script
1. What would you want to test by Christmas that you cannot test today?
2. What did the last peak-season checkout incident actually cost?holding: checkout failures at peak sit in the risk factors two years running3. What has to be true by end of Q3 for Basecamp to be called on track?holding: the direct-to-consumer target was set publicly at her hire
Drop
Online grew 31% while stores fell 4% last quarter. Your CFO said the current platform "limits how fast we can test." Peak-period constraints appear in the risk factors two years running.
Give
The ninety-day migration pattern comparable DTC brands followed, and what their first post-fix peak season measured (seller knowledge, prepare before the meeting).
Watch out
Do not cite the old "one third of revenue" goal as current: it predates her arrival, and no current source repeats it.
Timing
Act now. A new CDO six weeks in, with hiring stacked behind her. Next catalyst: the H1 report, late August.
Next
A working session with Solheim and the platform project's owner before the H1 report; fallback: she names that owner for a technical intro.

Sixteen reality checks run before delivery · guesses wear the [H] badge · the dossier and evidence table follow the card

Real prep takes 40 minutes. You have 10.

  • You open the meeting invite 10 minutes before the call.
  • Their website says "AI-powered platform" and nothing else.
  • You guess at their priorities and open with a discovery question they have answered forty times.
  • You lose the first five minutes proving you did no homework.

What the kit does

What comes back

Ten lines you can say out loud

The card you read in the lift, then the dossier: the money, what just changed, who is in the room, the tool you would be replacing. It closes with five to seven angles to open with.

What it reads

39 signals, three questions each

Filings, hiring, leadership moves, tech changes, expansion, reviews. Each signal gets the same three questions: is it my problem, is it fresh, who owns the decision. Then a verdict: act now, this week, or watch.

Why you can trust it

It grades its own work

Sixteen checks against stale or invented findings, run before you see a word, and it says so when it is guessing. It never reads LinkedIn. It sells nothing inside your prep. The whole method is public, on one page.
