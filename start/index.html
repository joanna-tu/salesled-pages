TARGET read as: Nordvik Outdoor ASA, the Oslo-listed outdoor retailer. Not Nordvik Marine AS, the boat dealer.
Meeting: Ingrid Solheim, Chief Digital Officer · Seller: Cartesa

Meeting card

The bet: The group bought Bretun for 240 million kroner in January and now runs two outdoor brands on two entirely separate commerce stacks. Nobody has said publicly how that resolves, and the person who will have to live with the answer is sitting across the table.

Ask (menu, not script):

  1. Where does Bretun's online sit in your scope now?holding: Bretun closed in January and did about eight and a half million kroner online in its first quarter inside the group
  2. What would have to be true before Bretun and Nordvik share one customer view?holding: the two brands run separate storefronts and separate accounts today
  3. What makes the second brand harder to grow online than the first?holding: online was under a sixth of group revenue in March

Drop: The Danish app went live under two weeks ago, on fourteen stores built there in under three years. Outdoor gear grew thirty-one percent last quarter, ahead of the group's twenty-one. In Bretun's first quarter inside the group, about a fifth of its revenue came from online, above the group average.

Give: the sequencing pattern comparable multi-brand retail groups follow after acquiring a brand with its own stack. What they answered first, what they deliberately left alone for eighteen months, and the event that forced the decision. Seller knowledge, prepare before the meeting.

Watch out: Do not call the current setup old, legacy, or a constraint. Her team shipped the Danish site and the Danish app on it, the last one thirteen days ago.

Timing: ACT NOW. Freshest signal is the Danish app launch, attached directly to her function. Next catalyst: the H1 report on 20 August, eight days out. Budget year is calendar; 2026 capex was set at roughly 95 million kroner across both segments, so the 2027 line item gets shaped this autumn. Note: the group is in a closed period until 20 August, so she cannot discuss unpublished numbers.

Next: a working session with her and whoever owns the Bretun platform decision, before the H1 report. Fallback: she names who owns Bretun's e-commerce stack.

Dossier

Legend: [F] verified fact with source · [H] my labeled guess · [C] sources disagree, do not quote · [S] scoring math. All resolve in EVIDENCE at the bottom.

Thesis

Nordvik is winning. Outdoor gear is the growth engine of a three billion kroner group, Denmark went from nothing to fourteen stores plus a native app in under three years, and the platform underneath it has been delivering. The tension is not performance. It is that the group spent 240 million kroner in January on a second brand, and that brand arrived carrying its own commerce stack, its own customer base and its own international storefronts.

  • The acquisition is now an online question. Bretun's online was already about a fifth of its own revenue in its first quarter, ahead of the group's 15.8 percent [F1][F2]. The earn-out runs on Bretun's future results, so someone is now measured on a channel that sits on a stack Nordvik does not run [F3].
  • The stack is split, and the split is verifiable. nordvik.no, nordvik.dk and both mobile apps share one headless core and one API [F5]. bretun.no is a mainstream SaaS storefront with separate accounts, plus its own .com and .de sites [F7]. Nothing published says which way that resolves.
  • The freshest news proves capability, not appetite. The Danish app shipped on 29 July [F5]. That is a team mid-roadmap with budget flowing: a reason to be in the room, and a reason not to arrive with a replacement story.

The problem, in their words: "The outdoor segment is our growth engine, but we now run two brands on two stacks, and every quarter we wait costs us the customer view that made the Bretun case in the first place." An insider should nod, not squint [H].

POVs

Suggested order: open cold with POV 1 and POV 2, both diagnostic. POV 4 is the most provocative; test it below her level first.

1. The acquisition's return is now a channel question. The group did not buy 22 boutiques for 240 million kroner. It bought a younger customer who was already buying online at a higher rate than the group average. Which means the return on that price is being decided in a channel, not in a store [H].

  • Move: name the unspoken pain
  • Ammo: "streng kostnadsdisiplin" as the CEO's own frame, against a 240 million bet whose upside is an earn-out
  • First test: a marketing or performance lead below her

Five more follow: two brands, two customers, one target · Denmark already answered the hard question · personalisation is an operations problem in a catalogue costume · the third market breaks the model · cost rigour and channel growth are the same sentence.
