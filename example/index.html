Thesis

Aurelin is winning. Jewellery is the growth engine of a nine billion crown group, Austria went from nothing to twelve stores plus a native app in under three years, and the platform underneath it has been delivering. The tension is not performance. It is that Karona spent 690 million crowns in January to buy a second jewellery brand, and that brand arrived carrying its own commerce stack, its own customer base and its own international storefronts. Two brands, two platforms, one segment P&L. The three angles:

  • The acquisition is now an online question. Amelot's online was already almost a quarter of its own revenue in its first quarter inside the group, ahead of the group's 16.2 percent [F1][F2]. The earn-out runs on Amelot's future results, which means someone is now measured on growing a channel that sits on a stack Aurelin does not run [F3].
  • The stack is split, and the split is verifiable. aurelin.cz, aurelin.at, both mobile apps and darino.cz share one headless core and one API [F5]. amelot.cz is a Magento-family build, plus separate international storefronts [F7]. Nothing published says which way that resolves.
  • The freshest news proves capability, not appetite. The Austrian app shipped on 30 July [F5]. That is a team mid-roadmap with budget flowing, which is a reason to be in the room and a reason not to arrive with a replacement story.

The problem, in their words: "The jewellery segment is our growth engine, but we now run two brands on two stacks, and every quarter we wait costs us the customer view that made the Amelot case in the first place." An insider should nod, not squint [H].

POVs

Grown from the thesis angles. Suggested order: open cold with POV 1 and POV 2, both diagnostic. Hold POV 3 and POV 5 until a reply. POV 4 is the most provocative; test it below his level first.

1. The acquisition's return is now a channel question. Karona did not buy a boutique network for 690 million crowns. It bought a younger customer who was already buying online at a higher rate than the group average. Which means the return on that price is being decided in a channel, not in a store [H].

  • Move: name the unspoken pain
  • Built on: [F2] Amelot online 21m of 92m in Q1 · [F1] group online 16.2% in March · [F3] 690m plus earn-out
  • Ammo: "přísná nákladová disciplína" as the CEO's own frame for 2026, against a 690 million crown bet whose upside is an earn-out
  • First test: a marketing or performance lead below him

2. Two brands, two customers, one segment target. The jewellery segment is now reported as one number and run as two customer bases. Every quarter that stays true, the group is paying twice to learn the same thing about the same person [H].

  • Move: find the common root
  • Built on: [F5] Aurelin's estate on one shared API · [F7] amelot.cz on its own build with its own accounts · [F1] jewellery reported as one segment
  • Ammo: their own segment language, "klenotnický segment", set against two entirely separate account systems
  • First test: Holan directly; it is a question about his reporting line, not his architecture

3. Austria already answered the hard question. Twelve stores and a localised app in under three years proves the group can enter a market fast when the technical foundation is shared. The constraint is no longer whether they can. It is which brand gets to go next [H].

  • Move: raise the ceiling
  • Built on: [F5] twelve AT stores in under three years, AT app on the shared API · [F7] Amelot already running amelot.com and amelot.de
  • Ammo: the 30 July post's own framing, that entering a new market did not require building separate infrastructure
  • First test: Holan, after a reply. It compliments his work before it asks anything

4. Personalisation is an operations problem in a catalogue costume. Nearly four thousand configurable items with engraving and initials is not a merchandising decision. It is a set of promises made at checkout that someone in a workshop has to keep, and the catalogue is where those two systems meet or fail [H].

  • Move: flip the status quo
  • Built on: [F7] 3,900 items on amelot.cz, heavy configurable-product use, engraving and initial attributes
  • Ammo: their own configurator language, "Cena podle zvolených variant", on almost every product
  • First test: never open with this. Test it on an operations or product manager first

5. The second market is the one that will break the model. Two markets on one core is elegant. Three is where the shared foundation either compounds or starts costing more than it saves, and the decision that determines which one happens gets made before the third market, not during it [H].

  • Move: plant the future problem
  • Built on: [F5] two markets, two apps, closed per-market customer bases · [F15, 2025] acquisition-led growth as the stated strategy
  • Ammo: the per-market closed customer base, described as a regulatory and corporate-structure requirement, not a design preference
  • First test: Holan, after a reply. It is a strategy question, not a platform question

6. Cost rigour and channel growth are the same sentence. Management said the rest of 2026 is sales growth under strict cost discipline. In a two-stack estate those are not two goals in tension. They are the same goal, and the number that reconciles them is what the second stack costs to run [H].

  • Move: find the common root
